## Overview

The loans UI is behind the `loans` beta flag (off by default, Settings → Beta). The flag hides only the UI: loan data, sync, the wallet type, and the delete cascade work either way.

Two wallet types make up credit products and answer different questions; `isCreditProduct` covers both:

- **Credit card** - a wallet with `type: 'credit_card'`. Covers revolving debt (credit cards, BNPL) that has no fixed schedule. Tracked through its own transactions (payments in, interest and fees as its expenses), the card figures the bank reports in `bank_reports`, `wallets.credit_limit`, and "due this month".
- **Loan** - a wallet with `type: 'loan'` and a `loans` row on top: an amortized contract with a generated payment schedule, optional overrides, and the reconciliation/summary engine in `app/app/components/loans/engine`.

Both are credit products, but only a loan has a schedule: a credit card has no `loans` row and reports only the minimum payment its bank states.

All amounts below are integers in the minor units of the wallet's currency; days are civil days (UTC-midnight ms). See [Data Model](/en/reference/data-model).

## Entities

### `loans`

One row per loan contract, and the **loan id is the wallet id**: a deterministic id, so two offline devices creating the same loan land on one row. Fields (`app/app/components/loans/types.ts`, `loanItemSchema`; columns in snake_case):

| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| `walletId` | The credit wallet; in the database it is the row's `id`, not a column |
| `principalAmount`, `annualRatePercent`, `termMonths` | Contract terms. `annualRatePercent` is `null` when the contract states no rate (`0` is a real interest-free loan); the engine then projects generated rows, planned interest and what-if at the rate the paid interest (or the bank schedule) implies, and hides what-if when there is neither |
| `startDate`, `firstPaymentDate`, `closedDate` | Civil days; `firstPaymentDate` is stated by the contract, never derived from `startDate`; `closedDate` is set when the user marks the loan closed |
| `paymentDayOfMonth` | 1-31, clamped to the length of each month |
| `scheduleType` | `annuity` or `differentiated` |
| `prepaymentMode` | `reduce_payment` or `reduce_term` - how the unpaid tail reacts to a prepayment |
| `interestAccrual` | `monthly` (rate / 12) or `daily` (actual/365, like most banks) |
| `lateAfterDays` | Days after the due date a payment still counts as on time (default in `loanItemSchema`) |
| `prepaymentWindowDays` | A payment more than this many days before the next due date is a prepayment (default in `loanItemSchema`) |
| `paymentWalletId` | Wallet the payment is debited from (optional, enables a "not enough money" signal) |
| `contractNumber`, `description` | Free text |

### `loan_schedule_overrides`

Only rows that differ from the generated schedule are stored - an untouched payment is represented by its absence. Id `<loanId>:<paymentNumber>` (`loanScheduleOverrideIdFor`). Fields (`loanScheduleOverrideSchema`): `loanId`, `paymentNumber`, `dueDate`, `principalAmount`, `interestAmount`, `totalAmount`, `source` (`bank` or `manual`).

### `bank_reports`

The figures the bank shows for a wallet live in `bank_reports`, one row per wallet (id = the wallet id), entered by hand or written through the API. Every field is optional, and each feature falls back when it is absent:

| Fields | Used by | Without them |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| `minPaymentAmount`, `minPaymentDueDate`, `minPaymentPeriodStartDate` | The card's minimum-payment line, reminders, "due this month" | No minimum-payment line or reminder |
| `graceDebtAmount`, `graceEndDate`, `statementDate` | The card's interest-free period line | No grace line |
| `debtAmount`, `debtReportDate` | `bankDebtCheck`: the loan wallet against the debt the bank reports | No reconciliation against the bank debt |
| `nextPaymentDate`, `nextPrincipalAmount`, `nextInterestAmount` | The next payment amount, when the date matches the next schedule row | The generated schedule's next payment |

### No foreign keys

There are no foreign keys between `loans`, `loan_schedule_overrides`, `bank_reports`, `transactions` and `wallets`, and no unique constraints across rows: an offline write that lands out of order would be rejected and dropped from the upload queue. Uniqueness comes from the deterministic ids. Deleting a wallet deletes its loan (with its schedule overrides) and its `bank_reports` row.

## Invariants

- A payment is **one transfer into** the credit wallet for the whole amount, from any wallet. A transfer out of a credit wallet is spending on the product; only the to side is collected into the loan ledger (`collectCreditTrns` in `app/app/components/loans/engine/ledger.ts`).
- **Interest and fees** are what the bank takes out of the payment: ordinary `expense` transactions **of the credit wallet itself** with `categoryId: 'loan_interest'` or `categoryId: 'loan_fine'`. The principal repaid is payment - interest - fees, so the credit wallet balance stays the negative principal left.
- Example: a 12 500 payment with 9 800 interest is `Debit card -> Car loan` 12 500 plus a `loan_interest` expense of 9 800 on `Car loan`. Transfer exactly what the bank debits: money sent more than `prepaymentWindowDays` ahead of the due date reads as a prepayment. Amounts are exact to the minor unit.
- `loan_interest` and `loan_fine` are **reserved** category ids (`isReservedCategoryId` in `app/app/components/categories/utils.ts`) without a `categories` row; their look is edited through `display_overrides`, and they are never deleted. They are ordinary expenses and **do count** in income/expense statistics.
- A credit wallet's balance is negative debt. Anything the loan engine calls "remaining" reads `-(walletBalance)`.

## Engine pipeline

Pure functions in `app/app/components/loans/engine`, composed in `derive.ts`:

1. **`generateSchedule(params, overrides)`** (`schedule.ts`) - builds the row-by-row plan (annuity or differentiated), splicing in `overrides` (rows sourced from a bank statement or entered by hand) in place of the generated ones. A holiday row (nothing due, `totalAmount <= 0 && principalPart <= 0`) pushes the term one month further out instead of shrinking it. Interest is `rate / 12` a month (`monthly`), except a first period that is not exactly a month from `startDate`, which accrues daily; `daily` accrues actual/365 over every period. The annuity payment is solved over the whole schedule (overrides, holidays, short periods included), so the last row never absorbs a gap.
2. **`reconcileSchedule(params, rows, trns, today)`** (`reconcile.ts`) - matches real transactions onto schedule rows sequentially (transactions by date, rows by number), exact in minor units. A `payment` adds to the row's principal, `interest` and `fine` add to their part and are taken out of its principal, whichever lands first. Interest or a fine on the day of the row's payment always stays in that row. A row is covered when its principal OR its whole amount is paid: a bank re-accrues interest by days, so on a bank row the principal matches and the total moves, on a generated row it is the other way round. Bank and manual rows must match exactly; a generated row is our estimate and allows 1% short. Every transaction lands in exactly one row. When the cursor leaves a row, what it got beyond its total (only when the principal was overpaid too, so a payment without its interest or a little extra interest is not a prepayment) first pays the following rows already due on that day (a skipped month caught up, with no transactions of its own). The balance then follows the principal actually repaid (a payment without its interest is assumed to have paid the planned interest), and any deviation re-solves the unpaid tail through `rebuildTail` right away, so minor units carry into the tail and later payments are matched against the plan in force. The one date window: a payment more than `prepaymentWindowDays` before the next due date, with the previous row closed, is a prepayment on its own day and never opens a row; the interest or fine charged with it that day goes with it. Produces a `status` per row: `scheduled`, `paid`, `partial`, `overdue`, or `late` (paid more than `lateAfterDays` after the due date).
3. **`summarizeLoan(params, rows, walletBalance)`** (`summary.ts`) - totals paid/planned principal, interest and fines; computes `effectiveRate` (paid interest annualized over the average real opening balance it was charged on - answers "what did this loan actually cost", not a restatement of the contract rate) `unrecognized` (the gap between the wallet balance and the reconciled principal; 0 when everything matches), `interestMissing` (settled payments recorded without their interest), `projectionRate`, and `nextPayment` as what is still owed on the first unpaid row.
4. **`whatIf(params, rows, extra, mode)`** (`summary.ts`) - interest saved and new end date if `extra` went to the principal right now, starting from the real debt and re-solving the unpaid tail as if it were freshly generated. The re-solved interest is scaled by how far the re-solve is off the planned interest of that tail (a bank plan accrues by days), so no extra saves 0 and paying everything off saves the whole planned interest.
5. **`derivePortfolio(input)`** (`portfolio.ts`) - everything the loans page needs across all loans in one pass, converted into the base currency: totals, an avalanche pick (largest interest saved) and a snowball pick (smallest remaining balance) for one extra payment.

`rebuildTail` (in `schedule.ts`) is the shared re-solver behind both overpayment reconciliation and `whatIf`: `reduce_payment` re-solves the payment over the same row count, `reduce_term` keeps the payment and runs out of balance earlier, dropping rows that are no longer needed. Bank and manual rows are never recalculated, only re-chained onto the running balance; `reduce_payment` solves the payment over the whole tail, so bank rows in it keep their own principal instead of the last generated row absorbing it.

`ledger.ts` also exposes `costOf(trns)`, which totals interest and fees for one credit wallet and buckets them by civil month - used for both loans and plain revolving wallets.

## Where things live

- **Pure engine**: `app/app/components/loans/engine/{types,schedule,reconcile,summary,ledger,derive,portfolio}.ts`. No Vue, no store, no I/O.
- **Store**: `app/app/components/loans/useLoansStore.ts`. Holds `items` (loans) and `scheduleOverrides` state, watches the `loans` and `loan_schedule_overrides` PowerSync tables, and exposes computeds (`byWalletId`, `costByWalletId`, `dueThisMonth`, `trnsByCreditWallet`) that call the pure engine functions with live store data. CRUD (`saveLoan`, `deleteLoan`, `saveScheduleOverride`, `deleteScheduleOverride`) follows the same optimistic-update-then-`upsertRow`/`deleteRow` pattern as the other stores.
- **Views vs containers**: per `AGENTS.md`, `*View.vue` components under `app/app/components/loans/` (e.g. `ScheduleView`, `SummaryView`, `CostView`, `PortfolioView`, `RecommendationView`, `RevolvingView`, `CardLineView`, `WhatIfView`) are pure - props in, emits out, JSON-serializable props. `Page.vue` is the container: it owns `useLoansStore`, `useWalletsStore`, `useCurrenciesStore`, converts between currencies, and passes plain values down to the views.
- **Minimum payment**: `app/app/components/loans/minPayment.ts` derives a revolving wallet's due/overdue/paid/stale status from the minimum-payment fields of its `bank_reports` row and the payments into the wallet since the billing period started.
- **Wallet page figures**: `principalFree.ts` flags calendar months with no principal repaid (interest-only or a holiday row); `engine/bankDebt.ts` compares the wallet with `bank_reports.debt_amount` and goes stale once a payment fell due after the bank figure. `presenters.ts` maps both, plus the overpayment (paid interest and fines as a share of principal) and an effective rate shown only when it differs from the contract rate by more than 0.5 pp, into `SummaryView`/`ScheduleView` props.
- **"Due this month"**: `deriveDue` in `derive.ts`, called from the store's `dueThisMonth` computed. Merges every overdue or partial row of each loan (what is still owed) plus its next scheduled payment with each revolving wallet's minimum payment over a rolling 31-day window, converted to the base currency.

## Testing

- Unit tests: every `app/app/components/loans/engine/*.test.ts`, one per engine module.
- Fixtures under `app/app/components/demo/loans/`: `loan-{a,b,c}.json` are anonymized bank schedules (amounts and rates rescaled, dates shifted) that keep the banks' real behavior (daily interest, holidays, early payoff); the rest feed the view tests.
- View tests: every `app/app/components/loans/**/*.view.test.ts`, one per pure view, mounted from fixtures per the `View` testing rule in `AGENTS.md`.
- Store and helper tests: the remaining `*.test.ts` under `app/app/components/loans/`.
